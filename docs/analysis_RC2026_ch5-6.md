## 5. Observations (descriptive)

1. **5.1 is about funding conditions; 5.2 is about companies; chapter 6 is about coordination.** The vocabulary follows that split (risk and funding in 5.1, companies in 5.2, cooperation, ministries and monitoring in chapter 6).
2. **Risk-taking is treated on three levels:** researchers' careers, organisations' funding mix, and companies' risk capacity, plus a cultural level (leadership and organisational culture).
3. **Investment attraction targets large firms.** The two named targets are large internationally strong firms and Finnish multinationals keeping RDI in Finland. Start-ups appear once, in the sentence about firms willing to increase R&D.
4. **Implementation builds on existing structures.** No new body or instrument is created. Additional state funding for the choices is conditional ("erikseen niin päätettäessä").
5. **Business Finland and the Academy of Finland are named as the main implementers** (5 mentions each in chapter 6), mainly through funding calls and programme targeting.
6. **The monitoring areas have no targets, baselines or schedule** in these chapters. They are listed as examples, and the detailed monitoring is delegated to the 2025 monitoring plan.
7. **The only quantified commitments are the industry's own** (technology industry 2021; pharmaceuticals €1 billion by 2030). The first is explicitly conditional.
8. **The time frame extends beyond this government's term**, with each government able to deepen the choices through the multi-annual plan.

## 6. Interpretation (tentative questions, not findings)

- **Q1. Selectivity versus risk-taking.** Additional Business Finland funding goes to the companies that are "kyvykkäimpiä… riskinottoon". Does targeting the most capable companies widen risk-taking, or concentrate it? The text alone cannot answer this; funding data and evaluations would be needed.
- **Q2. Coordination as a recurring theme.** The call for "määrätietoisempaa… johtamista ja koordinaatiota" echoes the 2014 evaluation of the Council (AN2014), which found its position weakened. Is coordination a constant complaint across Council reviews? This is testable once the 2003–2017 reviews are in.
- **Q3. Choice without new resources.** Implementation relies on existing structures, with additional money conditional. How binding are the choices in practice? The monitoring plan (2025) and later budgets would show this.
- **Q4. Investment attraction as an RDI-system priority.** Attracting foreign and keeping domestic corporate RDI is framed as necessary for the 4% target. Compare with the 1990s–2000s emphasis on domestic innovation-system building.

These are questions about documents and institutions. None is a judgement of any organisation's performance or of any named person.

## 7. Note on the author's position

Business Finland is named in these chapters as a main implementer. The author works at Business Finland. This analysis uses only the public report, and it describes what the text says without evaluating Business Finland's role or performance. Public outputs based on it (posts, videos) should stay descriptive. Questions Q1–Q3 are research questions for the document corpus, not commentary on the agency.

## 8. Limitations

- **Two chapters of one document.** The counts are small and descriptive only.
- **The chapters refer to documents not yet in the corpus**: the 2025 monitoring and evaluation plan, the OECD reviews of 2017 and 2022, and Sitra (2024b).
- **The industry commitments are reported as the document reports them;** their delivery is not checked here.
- **Mixed language in the source.** The text is in Finnish and the excerpts are kept verbatim; the English summaries are mine.
